Contrapunctia is a micronation I founded on November 13th, 2025. It's official language is Interslavic (modified, see below) and it is located in my house and backyard. 

Contrapunctian Interslavic (also called "Contrapunctian" or "Contrapunctian Slavic") differs from Standard Interslavic in nine major ways: Firstly, like in Polish, perfect tense verbs are marked for person AND gender, including for plural forms (virility). Secondly, conditional and pluperfect tenses (that use the by and bě partcles in SIS) append the particles to the verb (also like in Polish). Thirdly, the imperfective future tense is synthetic and is marked with a -č- in place of the infinitive -ti with -e-type personal endings appended (like in South Slavic languages). Fourthly, the present tense conjugation of perfective verbs is always the future perfective tense, like in North Slavic languages. Fifthly, there are future participles (perfective present and imperfective/iterative future via -či in place of infinitive). Sixthly, the vocative case, dual number, and the past simple tense are mandatory. Seventhly, there is a Future-in-the-Past and a Future Perfect tense (formed by adding secondary endings or East Slavic present tense endings to the infinitive). Eighthly, there exists a highly complex, irregular subjunctive mood that is generally applicable to every tense in the language. Ninthly, there exists a productive causative, doubling every single form for most verbs. As a side note, aspectual variations of a single verb are counted as conjugations of the verb to more accurately reflect and scale the complexity of Slavic verbal morphology to systems used by Western languages, which often encode aspect via tense. All of this brings a verb's total conjugated forms to over 1300, excluding participles.

The three official scripts are Latin, Cyrillic, and Glagolitic. The one difference in orthography is that, like in Serbo-Croatian, the digraph "dž" is represented by its etymological orthography variant "đ" to optimise efficiency and make the orthography more symmetric (the voiceless postalveolar affricate is written "č", not "tš").
